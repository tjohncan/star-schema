# Almagest Atlas — payload specification (as written, July 2026)

A single self-contained HTML file: Ptolemy's 48 constellations,
one fixed scene at a time, drawn on a realistic night sky
and annotated from our own warehouse.

*NOTE this "spec" is a period piece, kept unedited.*
This is the plan the atlas was built from,
not a description of what it became. Some is superseded;
one promise is deliberately unkept.
Where this file and the code disagree,
the code is right — preserved here are the reasoning and history.

## Shape

- **One file**: `payloads/almagest_atlas/index.html`, generated by
  `build.py` from `template.html` + queries against `dev.duckdb`
  after a fresh `dbt build`. Data is **inlined** into the HTML
  (`window.ATLAS`) — the file opens from disk with a double-click,
  no server necessary (no network; no external libraries).
  The generated file is committed.
- **48 scenes + an index.** Each scene is a fixed camera; no free-flight.
  The index lists the constellations in Ptolemy's book order
  with English and Latin names and member counts.

## Experience

Two views. **Index**: a splash overlay listing all 48 constellations
(book order, both names, member counts)
shown when nothing is selected.
**Scene**: rendering on the left, text panel on the right,
a slim nav bar on top (prev / index / next).
Lots of text, deliberately arranged (by a LITERAL MACHINE - thanks Claude!).

- **Index panel** — the splash overlay: a frieze of the whole catalogue
  up top (every placed member and all 48 figures on one equirectangular
  band, east to the left, seamed at RA 71° where the fewest figures are
  cut — decorative, not interactive), then the constellation cards;
  click a constellation, get its scene.
  On a fresh load the Little Bear sits preloaded beneath the splash,
  so Index and the arrow keys have somewhere to go from the very
  first click.
- **Scene** — canvas sky render, plus a text panel:
  - The constellation's names, figure/unformed counts,
    and our museum **description prose** as the intro.
  - **Viewing-angle line**: "Looking from the Sun toward RA h:m / Dec ±d°,
    field N°" — with a one-time footnote that from the Sun and from Earth
    the stellar sky differs by less than an arcsecond
    (that is what parallax is), so the heliocentric framing is honest.
  - Sky stats: brightest member, nearest/farthest member with sane
    parallax, count of backdrop stars in frame.
- **Tap-first, three views per scene.** Hover is desktop garnish (tooltip,
  transient rings, stroke glow); every interaction is reachable by
  click/tap, so the page works where hover does not exist.
  - **Nothing selected** — the detail card shows a hint;
    tapping empty sky returns here.
  - **Star focus** — tap a star (in the render, or its name in the lists
    below): detail card with the **caption** (our standalone picture line)
    leading, then the book's English in quotes with the Latin underneath,
    the naming waterfall and carried keys (HR, HD, HIP, Baily), member
    type, Ptolemy's magnitude beside the modern V, spectral type, B−V,
    distance when parallax supports one — and, for members bearing an IAU
    proper name, the **WGSN etymology paragraph** (origin and language).
    Figure stars also list their **edges as tappable chips** —
    the only edge menu is through stars or the picture, by design.
    The selected star wears a persistent ring.
  - **Edge focus** — tap a stroke (or a chip): in place of star detail,
    the stroke's name and comment, then its member stars as a vertical
    selectable column — best name+caption per row, **in Ptolemy's order**
    (seq ascending, not walk order). The selected stroke stays lit.
- **Background: on | off** — a small switch in the sky's corner douses
  the naked-eye backdrop, leaving Ptolemy's stars and the figures alone
  on the black: the shape-reading mode. The choice holds across scenes;
  hidden backdrop stars stop answering taps, and a selected one is let go.
- **Panel lists** — two flowing lists close the panel:
  **Figured stars** and **Unformed stars (never edged)**,
  every name tappable into star focus.
  Arcturus floats free beside Boötes.
- **Unplaced stars** — any member we cannot place (no HIP, or no BSC match)
  appears in a short list in the panel ("not placed: N").

## Visual language

- Near-black sky, faint graticule optional (off by default).
- Stars: radius/glow from V magnitude (perceptual scale,
  brighter = larger + soft bloom);
  **tint from B−V** via a short blue→white→amber ramp —
  the sky gets its real colors.
- Figure stars at full strength; backdrop stars (all BSC stars in frame)
  present but restrained. The sky must read as a sky, not a diagram.
- Strokes: thin lines, low-contrast until hovered.
- Unformed stars: no edges; a dotted ring on hover
  to say "catalogued, unbound."
- Ptolemy's **nebulous entries** (magnitude code 9, Praesepe and friends):
  soft blurred patch, not a point.
- Look-and-feel brief: quiet, serious; typography delivers the charm!

## Camera model

- Per scene: center = centroid of the figure stars;
  **stereographic projection** about that center.
  In plain words: this is the "what your eyes would see" choice —
  near the center it matches a photograph aimed at the figure,
  and shapes stay honest as you move outward,
  which is the "night sky lined up toward the center of the constellation"
  desired. Distortion only creeps in at the far edges of the giant figures
  — Hydra's scene computes to ~150° and is the stress test to eyeball.
- Field of view = twice the maximal member offset × 1.3 margin,
  floored at 12° (computed in `atlas_constellation`); celestial north up.
- The camera never moves within a scene. Scene-to-scene is index
  navigation only.

## Data contract (`window.ATLAS`)

Named fields everywhere; no positional tuples.
Numbers pre-rounded at build time. JavaScript's only jobs:
project, draw, and look things up in maps — zero astronomy in the browser.

```
{
  meta:  { schema_version, counts, provenance, citations[] },
  stars: [ { hr, name, name_tier,            // best name + which tier it is
             proper?, bayer?, flamsteed?,    // waterfall pieces present
             cst, vmag, bv?, sp?,
             ra, dec,                        // degrees, render-ready
             dist_pc?                        // when parallax supports it
           } ],                              // all BSC stars (backdrop pool)
  members: [ { baily, hr?,                   // hr null => unplaced
               cst, member_type, seq,
               latin, english, caption,      // caption: figured stars only
               pt_mag, pt_qual?, id_quality } ],
  constellations: [ { code, name_latin, name_english, description,
                      view: { ra, dec, fov_deg },
                      stats: { ... },
                      strokes: [ { name, comment, seqs: [..] } ] } ]
}
```

- Strokes reference figure `seq`; a build-time map resolves
  (code, seq) → baily → hr so the JS never joins anything.
- xyz note: 3D positions derive from ra/dec/parallax in the warehouse
  (the star-bridge mart); the payload carries the angles it renders plus
  distance where meaningful. If the FTL future wants raw xyz vectors in
  the contract, that is a schema_version bump, not a redesign.

## Derivation responsibilities

- **prep_sources.py** carries catalogue facts verbatim
  (Hipparcos slice: hip, ra, dec, parallax, pm, hd
  — no derivation in prep).
- **dbt models** do all astronomy: the star bridge
  (baily → hip → hd → hr), the naming waterfall
  (WGSN proper → Bayer/greek seed → Flamsteed → HR), epoch handling,
  per-constellation centroids/fov/stats, stroke resolution.
  Every payload field traces to a ("gold") mart column.
- **build.py** formats only: query, round, assemble JSON,
  inject into `template.html` at a marked slot, write `index.html`.
- **Coverage tests** in dbt pin the honest numbers:
  how many members are placed (expect ~1,020+),
  that the 4 known HIP-less stars and any BSC-missing edge cases
  are exactly the unplaced list, all 48 scenes non-empty.

## Acceptance

- Opens from `file://`, no console errors, no network requests.
- All 48 scenes render; every figure star drawn or listed as unplaced.
- Hover/click behaviors above all work; Latin is reachable for every star.
- Usable at phone width: below 700 px the layout stacks
  (sky above, panel below) under the same tap-first interactions.
- Single file, target < ~2 MB.
- `py payloads/almagest_atlas/build.py` after a fresh `dbt build`
  reproduces the committed file byte-for-byte.

## Decisions log

1. **Projection**: stereographic, locked — it is the "as seen from Earth,
   pointed at the figure" rendering (explained above). Hydra is the
   distortion stress test to check at first render.
2. **Epoch**: resolved and implemented — `int_star_bridge` propagates
   Hipparcos J1991.25 positions to J2000.0 with the catalogue proper
   motions.
3. **Backdrop floor**: decide at first render; likely some magnitude floor
   rather than every point.
4. **Look-and-feel**: data-driven, as ruled — black sky + colored lights
   (B−V tints are real and carried) + sized/glowing by magnitude + quiet
   edges that brighten on hover. Nail the rest when it's in front of our
   eyes.
5. **Edge sets v2** (2026-07-20): the figure-QA pass landed — all
   self-crossings and catalogue-order bowties redrawn in the museum,
   Sagittarius re-legged, the Southern Fish made a fish. 336 strokes.
6. **Comment voice**: the strict `Anatomy:`/`License:` prefixes are
   retired; stroke comments are free prose, with our-line honesty inline
   where a connection is ours rather than the catalogue's.
7. **Captions**: the museum grew a standalone picture caption per figured
   star (relative references resolved); it leads the star detail card,
   annotates edge-focus rows, and rides the tooltip.
   Unformed stars carry none.
8. **Interaction model** (2026-07-20): tap-first with three views —
   nothing / star focus / edge focus — because hover is not
   mobile-friendly; hover remains as desktop garnish only.
9. **Background switch** (2026-07-20): each scene's corner carries
   `Background: on | off`. Off douses the backdrop for shape-reading;
   the state survives scene navigation, hidden stars leave hit-testing,
   and a selected backdrop star is released. Zoom was weighed for the
   same job and deliberately skipped: the switch solves it without
   touching the fixed-camera contract, and pinch fights tap-first
   selection — free zoom stays on the v2 queue.
10. **Splash frieze** (2026-07-20): the index opens with the whole
    catalogue on one thin equirectangular band — members only,
    east to the left, right edge at RA 71°
    (the meridian scan's minimum: 3 segments cut across 2 figures).
    Decorative by decision; the scenes stay the only interactive sky.
11. **Small screens** (2026-07-20): below 700 px the page stacks sky
    over panel (dvh-aware); hover was already garnish, so touch loses
    nothing. Tablets get the desktop layout.
